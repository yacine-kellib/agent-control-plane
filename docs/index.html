A credential is not authorisation.

Give an agent the keys and every document it reads holds the keys. ACP moves the authorise-or-refuse decision outside the model, where prompt injection cannot reach it. The model proposes. It never authorises.

The shape

The modelproposes · holds no keys
Policy enginerecomputes risk from signed policy the model cannot see
Approverssign the exact hash, where a quorum is required
Executorreleases on a verified receipt, or nothing happens

A compromised model can request a synthesis order as often as it likes and never cause one. The risk level is recomputed from signed policy the model never sees, and an irreversible action needs human signatures bound to that exact request.

What this does not claim

  • No independent adversarial review. Every suite here was written by the party that wrote the code. Agreement between two implementations is evidence about consistency, never about correctness.
  • Nothing runs end to end yet outside the simulation. The control plane services moved to a separate repository, so nothing here has a main() that could be mistaken for a running system - there is no longer anything to mistake.
  • Status: evaluate, not deploy. The residual risks are published before the positive claims, deliberately, and they are the part worth reading first.
